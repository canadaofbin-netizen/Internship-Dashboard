# UCAS Personal Statement & Candidate Strategic Narrative Dossier

## 1. Official Personal Statement Text

> When I first arrived in Canada, I felt that Starbucks lowered my self-esteem. The anxiety that my English would not be understood by locals, and the looks I received when the staff couldn't understand me, made me shrink. Although I had been interested in psychology, and theoretically knew that environmental changes could cause psychological changes, experiencing the environmental gap between Korea and Canada made me pursue psychology on a more formal basis. This experience sparked my curiosity about the various factors that influence human thinking.
>
> In science class, I completed a project that explored how much the brain is affected by environmental factors. I discovered that the size of the hippocampus and amygdala shows different patterns in PTSD patients, realising that the level of brain activation varies depending on external stimuli. Furthermore, I found that there are individual differences in brain activation, leading me to realise that innate human traits also influence thinking.
>
> In my psychology club, I read Choi In-cheol's book, *Frame*, which explains why bronze medalists are happier than silver medalists. It made me realize that the sense of accomplishment doesn't always correlate directly with the outcome. I initially thought counterfactual thinking was irrational, but after reading Neal J. Roese's book, *The Functional Basis of Counterfactual Thinking*, I learned that it serves a preparatory function for the future beyond mere regret. Additionally, by examining it through the T-F indicator of the MBTI, I also discovered that T types engage in upward counterfactual thinking more often than F types. This experience helped me gain an understanding of the multifaceted interpretation of thought and the complexity of the human psychological system.
>
> After reading an article stating that the divorce rate in Korea has exceeded 40%, I started to recognize the seriousness of the divorce issue. This can have a wider impact as John Gottman's book, *What Makes Love Last*, showed that divorce can cause trauma in the developmental processes of adolescents. I realised that statistical thinking is crucial for such analysis, and while studying mathematics, I developed an interest in data statistical analysis. In my probability and statistics class, I analysed the divorce and birth rates of my country and discovered that an increase in divorce rates can lead to a decrease in birth rates, which could potentially contribute to an economic crisis. My analysis won a gold medal in a Mathematics and Statistics Application Competition.
>
> One of the most fulfilling experiences of my school life was volunteering for two years in a suicide prevention club. The school counselor recommended me for the role due to my strong listening skills and approachable personality. I received training to understand the psychological and environmental factors affecting students and helped them connect with the counsellor. During difficult times, I sought counseling myself, learning the process firsthand. Overall, many of the students experienced significant anxiety due to academic and family issues, and I helped them find psychological stability. By empathizing and witnessing their moments of relief, I also experienced personal growth. Through this experience, I was able to apply theoretical knowledge to real-life situations, leading to significant mental and emotional development.
>
> The environmental contrasts between Korea and Canada have expanded my understanding of different strengths and weaknesses. In psychological therapy, I aspire to be a specialist who considers individual temperaments and environmental factors to tailor treatments. By studying psychology in the UK, I aim to observe my own growth in a new environment and embrace these differences to provide the best possible care to future clients.
